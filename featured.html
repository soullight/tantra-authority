Featured

A rotating editorial spotlight on the people, practices, and aesthetics this site cares about. Real interviews, real photography, real disclosure of lineage and work — done with the Tantra Authority editorial frame instead of the conventional men's-magazine frame.

The four recurring features

1. Featured Woman

A monthly cover-feature on a woman whose body, work, and sexual self-determination embody what this site advocates for. Not a centerfold. Not "the girl next door." A real adult: writer, artist, sex worker, dancer, model, somatic teacher, photographer, ritualist — whatever she is — with full editorial treatment. Long-form interview about her work, her lineage, her relationship to her own body and sexuality, her economics, her boundaries, her appetites. Photography by collaborators who treat the body as a subject, not a product.

The format is closer to a 1970s Playboy Interview crossed with a contemporary art-magazine cover story than to a current-era objectification. The woman speaks. Her work is documented. Her body is photographed honestly. The reader leaves understanding her, not consuming her.

2. Featured Fetish

Each issue, one specific fetish or practice gets the deep-treatment editorial workup. Featured Foot Fetish. Featured Submission. Featured Group Sex. Featured Voyeurism. Featured Exhibitionism. Featured Public Play. The same investigative pattern — neuroscience, evolutionary frame, dismantling of shame, cultural history — combined with a featured practitioner / artist / writer working in that lane, with their work showcased and their lineage disclosed.

This is not a kink-of-the-month listicle. It is sustained editorial attention to one piece of the human shadow at a time, treated with the seriousness it deserves and the lightness that prevents the seriousness from becoming pretentious.

3. Featured Artist

Erotic art, photography, ritual art, somatic art, performance art, body-based art — practitioners whose work intersects with what this site is about. Each feature: a portfolio of their work, a long-form interview about their practice, prints / books / commissions available through the shop, and the editorial context that shows the reader why this artist matters.

The point is to put the artists this site admires into a context where they are taken seriously as artists, not as decorators.

4. Featured Photographer

The same model as Featured Artist, narrowed to photographers — because erotic / body / fine-art photography is its own discipline with its own lineages and standards, and most modern erotic photography either pretends to be art and isn't, or actually is art and gets ghettoized into "boudoir" by a culture that doesn't know how to look at it.

Featured photographers get a dedicated portfolio spread, an interview about their craft, prints available through the shop, and the editorial context that distinguishes their work from the generic.
